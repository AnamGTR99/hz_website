# HUGOZBOR

## Internal Revenue Splits & Delegated Production Framework

**Status:** Active
**Effective:** 2026
**Audience:** Internal (Management, Hugo, Studio Collaborators)

---

## 1. Purpose of This Document

This document defines the **internal revenue split structure**, **delegation rules**, and **brand protection framework** for all projects operating under the Hugo Zbor brand.

Its purpose is to:

* Protect Hugo Zbor’s creative positioning
* Enable scalable execution without brand dilution
* Ensure fair, incentive-aligned compensation
* Avoid ambiguity in delegated work
* Centralize operational control under management

This document is **not client-facing**.

---

## 2. Core Roles

### Hugo Zbor

**Role:** Creative Director / Brand / Vision

* Owns the Hugo Zbor brand and creative identity
* Provides creative direction, taste, and final approval
* Selectively executes high-tier work personally

---

### Shei (Management)

**Role:** Talent Manager / Operator

* Manages inbound, pricing, negotiation, and scoping
* Protects Hugo’s time and positioning
* Oversees delivery, QA, timelines, and client comms
* Controls payment intake and internal distribution

---

### Lutfi (ZK Vision)

**Role:** Studio Execution Partner (Per-Project)

* Executes delegated video/editing work
* Operates under Hugo’s creative direction
* Paid only when explicitly involved
* No ownership of clients or inbound

---

## 3. Project Categories

All projects fall into one of the following categories.

---

### A. Standard Hugo Projects (No Delegation)

**Description:**

* Hugo executes the work personally
* No involvement from ZK Vision
* Typically higher-budget or high-visibility work

**Revenue Split:**

* **Hugo:** 70%
* **Management:** 30%

This split is **locked and unchanged**.

---

### B. Website Projects (No Delegation Ever)

**Description:**

* Website design, development, and digital experiences
* Hugo + Management only
* ZK Vision is never involved

**Revenue Split:**

* **Hugo:** 55%
* **Management:** 45%

This split is **locked and permanent**.

---

### C. Curated / Delegated Projects (ZK Vision Involved)

**Description:**

* Smaller-budget or execution-heavy projects
* Clients still buy Hugo’s taste and direction
* Execution is delegated to ZK Vision under supervision

Clients are never told they are “working with someone else.”
The framing is always **curated and directed by Hugo Zbor**.

---

## 4. Revenue Splits — Delegated Projects

### Default Delegated Split (Standard Use)

This is the **primary** and recommended split.

* **Hugo:** 35%
* **Management:** 25%
* **ZK Vision (Lutfi):** 40%

**Rationale:**

* Hugo is paid for creative direction and brand leverage
* Management is paid for sourcing, ops, QA, and risk
* ZK Vision receives the largest share due to execution labor

---

### Light-Hugo Variant (Exception Only)

Used only when:

* Hugo provides minimal input (kickoff + light review)

* No deep iteration or creative problem-solving

* Format is repeatable and clearly defined

* **Hugo:** 30%

* **Management:** 25%

* **ZK Vision:** 45%

This variant should be **rare**, not standard.

---

## 5. Non-Negotiable Internal Rules

1. **Hugo never drops below 30%**
   If a project cannot support this, it does not run under the Hugo Zbor brand.

2. **Management never drops below 25%**
   Management carries operational, reputational, and delivery risk.

3. **ZK Vision is paid per project only**
   No retainers, no standing percentages, no entitlement.

4. **Website projects are excluded from delegation**
   ZK Vision has zero participation.

5. **Payment always flows through management**
   Clients pay Hugo Zbor / Management.
   Internal payouts are distributed afterward.

---

## 6. Credit & Public Attribution Rules

When delegated work is posted or delivered:

**Approved credit format:**

* Creative Direction: Hugo Zbor
* Edit / Production: ZK Vision

Rules:

* ZK Vision may post the work
* Hugo must be tagged
* Clients are not positioned as “ZK Vision clients”
* Hugo reposts selectively at his discretion

---

## 7. Brand Protection & Control

* ZK Vision operates **under** the Hugo Zbor umbrella
* No direct client ownership is created through delegation
* Involvement is approved **per project**
* Management reserves the right to:

  * Decline delegation
  * Replace execution resources
  * Pause or terminate delegated workflows if quality slips

---

## 8. Future Expansion Rule

If additional collaborators are added (e.g. editors, 3D artists, assistants):

* They are paid **from the execution share**
* Hugo’s and Management’s percentages are not diluted
* No collaborator automatically receives ongoing access

---

## 9. Summary Table

| Project Type                  | Hugo | Management | ZK Vision |
| ----------------------------- | ---- | ---------- | --------- |
| Standard Hugo (No Delegation) | 70%  | 30%        | 0%        |
| Website Projects              | 55%  | 45%        | 0%        |
| Delegated – Default           | 35%  | 25%        | 40%       |
| Delegated – Light Hugo        | 30%  | 25%        | 45%       |

---

## 10. Final Note

This framework exists to:

* Scale output without lowering standards
* Protect long-term brand equity
* Maintain clear authority and accountability
* Ensure everyone is paid in proportion to value and risk

All exceptions must be **explicitly approved by management**.

# HUGOZBOR

## ZK Vision (Lutfi) — Framing, Usage & Client Positioning

**Status:** Active
**Effective:** 2026
**Audience:** Internal (Management, Hugo)

---

## 1. Strategic Purpose of ZK Vision

ZK Vision exists to:

* Absorb **execution-heavy, lower-budget work**
* Preserve Hugo Zbor’s creative energy for high-leverage projects
* Increase throughput **without brand dilution**
* Create a controlled growth pathway for a junior collaborator

ZK Vision is **not**:

* A sub-brand sold independently
* A cheaper alternative to Hugo
* A parallel studio competing for the same clients

ZK Vision is an **execution layer inside the Hugo Zbor umbrella**.

---

## 2. Brand Hierarchy (Non-Negotiable)

Correct hierarchy:

**Hugo Zbor**
→ Creative Direction / Vision / Taste

**ZK Vision (Lutfi)**
→ Studio Execution / Production

This hierarchy must be maintained in:

* Client calls
* Emails
* Decks
* Captions
* Credits
* Internal language

If the hierarchy blurs, **Hugo’s brand value erodes**.

---

## 3. When ZK Vision Is Used

ZK Vision is used **only** when all of the following are true:

### Budget Conditions

* Project budget is **below Hugo’s ideal execution threshold**
* Typically $200–$500 range

### Scope Conditions

* Format is repeatable or execution-heavy
* No experimental, brand-defining creative risk
* No flagship campaign or press-facing asset

### Client Conditions

* Client wants Hugo’s taste, not necessarily his hands
* Client is price-sensitive but brand-aligned
* Client would otherwise be declined

If any of these are not met → **Hugo-only or decline**.

---

## 4. When ZK Vision Is NOT Used

ZK Vision must **never** be used for:

* Website projects (ever)
* High-visibility brand campaigns
* Work Hugo is personally passionate about
* Projects likely to attract press, features, or industry attention
* Anything where creative failure would harm Hugo’s reputation

---

## 5. How This Is Pitched to Clients (Core Framing)

### The Golden Rule

Clients are **not** buying “Lutfi’s editing.”
They are buying **Hugo Zbor’s taste, oversight, and system**.

---

### Approved Client Framing (Primary)

Use language like:

> “For smaller-scope projects, Hugo offers a curated format where he leads the creative direction and oversees the final output, while execution is handled by his studio team. This allows us to maintain quality while working within tighter budgets.”

Key signals embedded:

* Hugo is still involved
* Quality is preserved
* Budget accessibility is intentional, not a downgrade

---

### Secondary Framing (If Budget Pushback Exists)

> “This option exists specifically for clients who want Hugo’s aesthetic and guidance, but don’t require full hands-on execution. It’s still reviewed and directed by Hugo from start to finish.”

This reinforces:

* Oversight
* Intentionality
* Control

---

## 6. Language That Must Never Be Used

Do **not** say:

* “This will be edited by someone else”
* “This is a cheaper version”
* “My assistant will do it”
* “Hugo won’t really be involved”

These phrases:

* Devalue Hugo
* Reframe the offer as compromise
* Invite price negotiation

---

## 7. How Hugo Is Positioned in Delegated Projects

Hugo’s role must always be clear and respected:

Hugo:

* Joins the kickoff call
* Defines references and creative direction
* Sets the visual standard
* Reviews the final output

Even if time spent is minimal, **the authority must be visible**.

---

## 8. ZK Vision’s Visibility & Credit

ZK Vision is visible **only after** the hierarchy is established.

Correct credit order:

* Creative Direction: Hugo Zbor
* Edit / Production: ZK Vision

ZK Vision:

* May post the work
* Must tag Hugo
* Must not position the client as “his client”
* Operates as studio credit, not lead creative

---

## 9. Internal Mental Model (Important)

Think of ZK Vision as:

* A controlled execution extension
* A pressure valve for inbound
* A training ground under real standards
* A system you can scale or shut off

Not as:

* A partnership of equals
* A co-branded studio
* A long-term entitlement

---

## 10. Long-Term Strategic Benefit

This framing allows you to:

* Say “yes” without saying “cheap”
* Maintain Hugo’s upward pricing trajectory
* Capture revenue you’d otherwise reject
* Protect creative energy
* Train talent inside your ecosystem

---

## 11. Kill-Switch Rule

At any point, if:

* Quality drops
* Deadlines slip
* Clients get confused about who they’re working with

Management can:

* Pause all delegated work
* Remove ZK Vision from the pipeline
* Revert to Hugo-only projects immediately

No renegotiation required.

---

## 12. Final Principle

ZK Vision exists **to protect Hugo Zbor**, not replace him.

If a decision ever benefits ZK Vision at the expense of Hugo’s brand equity, it is the wrong decision.

## Project Acceptance & Delegation Decision Flowchart

**Status:** Active
**Effective:** 2026
**Audience:** Internal (Management)

---

## 1. Purpose

This flowchart defines **how every inbound lead is evaluated**, accepted, delegated, or declined under the Hugo Zbor brand.

The goal is to:

* Protect Hugo’s time and brand equity
* Maintain consistent decision-making
* Avoid emotional or reactive project acceptance
* Ensure delegation is intentional, not default

---

## 2. High-Level Decision Flow (Text Diagram)

```
INBOUND LEAD
     |
     v
Is the client brand-aligned?
     |
     |-- NO --> DECLINE (or refer externally)
     |
     |-- YES
            |
            v
Is this a WEBSITE project?
            |
            |-- YES --> WEBSITE PIPELINE
            |           (Hugo + Management only)
            |
            |-- NO
                   |
                   v
Is the budget at or above Hugo’s ideal execution threshold?
                   |
                   |-- YES
                   |       |
                   |       v
                   |   Does Hugo want to personally execute?
                   |       |
                   |       |-- YES --> HUGO-ONLY PROJECT
                   |       |
                   |       |-- NO  --> CURATED / DELEGATED PROJECT
                   |
                   |-- NO
                          |
                          v
Is the scope repeatable & execution-heavy?
                          |
                          |-- NO --> DECLINE
                          |
                          |-- YES
                                 |
                                 v
Is the client specifically requesting Hugo’s taste (not just price)?
                                 |
                                 |-- NO --> DECLINE
                                 |
                                 |-- YES --> CURATED / DELEGATED PROJECT
```

---

## 3. Step-by-Step Interpretation

### Step 1: Brand Alignment Check

**Ask:**

* Does this client match Hugo’s aesthetic, culture, or long-term trajectory?
* Would this work look out of place on Hugo’s portfolio or Instagram?

**If NO:**
→ Decline politely or refer externally.

**If YES:**
→ Continue.

---

### Step 2: Website Check (Hard Split)

**Ask:**

* Is this a website, landing page, or digital experience?

**If YES:**
→ Website pipeline only
→ Hugo + Management
→ ZK Vision never involved

**If NO:**
→ Continue.

---

### Step 3: Budget Threshold

**Ask:**

* Is the budget strong enough to justify Hugo executing personally?

**If YES:**
→ Ask Hugo if he *wants* to execute

* If yes → Hugo-only project
* If no → Curated / Delegated project

**If NO:**
→ Continue.

---

### Step 4: Scope Evaluation (Critical)

**Ask:**

* Is the work execution-heavy?
* Is the format repeatable?
* Is creative risk low?

**If NO:**
→ Decline (do not force delegation)

**If YES:**
→ Continue.

---

### Step 5: Client Intent Check

**Ask:**

* Do they want Hugo’s aesthetic and guidance?
* Or are they purely price-shopping?

**If Price-Shopping:**
→ Decline

**If Brand-Aligned & Direction-Focused:**
→ Curated / Delegated project

---

## 4. Outcome Definitions

### HUGO-ONLY PROJECT

* Hugo executes personally
* Standard 70 / 30 split
* No delegation

---

### WEBSITE PROJECT

* Hugo + Management only
* 55 / 45 split
* No exceptions

---

### CURATED / DELEGATED PROJECT

* Hugo provides direction + review
* ZK Vision executes
* Approved split applied
* Framed under Hugo Zbor umbrella

---

### DECLINE

* Polite, professional, fast
* No justification beyond availability or fit
* Protects long-term positioning

---

## 5. Internal Guardrails

* Delegation is **intentional**, not automatic
* Declining is often the correct move
* Lower budget ≠ lower standards
* If unsure, default to **decline or Hugo-only**, never forced delegation

---

## 6. Kill-Switch Reminder

If at any point:

* Client confusion increases
* Quality slips
* Hugo’s name is mispositioned

→ Delegated pipeline is paused immediately.

---

## 7. Final Principle

Every project must either:

* Increase Hugo Zbor’s brand equity
* Or be neutral while generating clean revenue

Anything else is rejected.
